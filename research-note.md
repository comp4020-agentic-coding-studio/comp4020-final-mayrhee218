<h1>Understanding AI Agents: Benefits and Limitations, and How We Can Create a Better Experience When Working with AI Agents</h1>

First, I realised that I needed to understand exactly what an AI agent is and how it differs from a conventional AI chatbot.

AI agents are different from the AI chatbots we commonly use through web interfaces, such as ChatGPT or Gemini. According to Gutowska (n.d.), AI agents are systems that autonomously perform tasks by planning workflows and using available tools. Unlike conventional AI chatbots, which mainly provide text-based responses, AI agents can make decisions, solve problems, and interact with external systems. They can also process various types of data, including user prompts, computer files, and real-time sensor data.

Although there are many dystopian predictions about artificial intelligence, I believe that AI can be a highly useful tool when used appropriately. Research also suggests that AI agents have significant potential to improve productivity. For example, Arnon (2025), in a report published by the Penn Wharton Budget Model at the University of Pennsylvania, The Projected Impact of Generative AI on Future Productivity Growth, estimates that generative AI could increase the level of total factor productivity (TFP) by approximately 1.5% by 2035, 3% by 2055, and 3.7% by 2075 compared with a scenario without AI.

Personally, this course was my first experience using an AI coding agent, as I had previously used AI primarily through chatbot interfaces.

I found the development process much faster and more efficient because the AI agent could directly modify files in my project. It was also able to understand my prompts even when my English grammar was not perfect. This made the development process more accessible and allowed me to focus more on developing ideas rather than spending time correcting language or manually editing every file.

It also encouraged me to try a project that I had never even considered before using AI. My project was a short-form video platform featuring 3D virtual avatars. I do not have much experience with 3D or video platforms, but the AI agent edited the project files, so I was able to finish my project even though I did not have specific knowledge of those fields (3D, virtual humans, and motion capture).

However, I also identified two important limitations that need to be addressed.

The website design generated by the AI was somewhat lacking in aesthetics and ease of use. For example, I gave the AI TikTok's UI as an example, and it adapted the feed well. However, for the "record" section (where users record videos), the UI was not very intuitive to use.

I've read an article by Rosala and Banawa (2026) from Nielsen Norman Group. According to their survey of 521 UX practitioners, 54% of respondents were concerned about the quality of AI's output.

(I'd personally like to investigate later why AI cannot always produce good UX and how to develop AI specialised in UX.) AI agents do not always understand human design preferences or what makes a UI intuitive and visually appealing. Therefore, repeated human feedback and clear descriptions of visual designs are necessary to improve the UI.

The other limitation was cybersecurity and privacy.

These issues require careful consideration when using AI agents. Because AI agents may access local files, modify code, or interact with external systems, they can introduce risks if their permissions and actions are not properly controlled. Developers should therefore review AI-generated changes, protect sensitive information, and limit access to unnecessary resources. While I was using the agents, they sometimes changed files that I did not want to change, which made me panic. A better way to use AI agents safely is to adjust their permission settings. Instead of using automatic mode, use manual or planning mode so that you can check the code and see which files will be edited or deleted.
